## A sustainable society

A sustainable society is a society where people can live a good life, while society's development fits within the planet's ecological limits.

For TSS, a sustainable society is not a finished state or a point that society one day reaches. It is a direction for the development of society – a striving towards a society where people's needs can be met, where people have the opportunity to influence their lives and their society, and where development takes place within the planet's ecological limits.

The vision of a sustainable society gives a shared direction. It can bring together people with different experiences, roles and views around something that is fundamentally hard to oppose: developing a society that is sustainable.

*It is hard to imagine anyone having the goal of developing an unsustainable society.*

## The vision as the foundation of TSS

The vision is the core and common thread of TSS. It gives a shared starting point for understanding today's society, identifying where the systems fall short and exploring which changes can steer development in a more sustainable direction.

The vision is not only about what society we want, but also about how we develop it. Society's organisation, economy and political decisions need to help meet people's needs and keep development within the planet's ecological limits.

That means we cannot assume that more production and economic growth are always desirable. Instead we need to ask what society needs to achieve and how activities and resources can be organised to contribute to a sustainable society.

A sustainable society needs to give people basic security and the opportunity to contribute to what we share. People's livelihoods should not have to depend on them working in activities that work against sustainable development. How society organises work, livelihoods and shared responsibility therefore becomes part of the transition.

It is not for this page to decide exactly how social security systems or working life should be designed. It is about a basic direction: society needs to organise the economy, work and shared resources so that people's needs can be met in a sustainable way.

## The vision has the support of the UN and of national constitutions

The vision is not something TSS has invented. It has already been set out by those who have the mandate to do so, both internationally and nationally.

Internationally, it is expressed in the UN Sustainable Development Goals, the SDGs, which are part of Agenda 2030. Agenda 2030 was adopted by all UN member states in 2015. The SDGs give an internationally anchored basis for describing what development towards a sustainable society needs to cover.

Nationally, it is found in many countries' constitutions and laws. Sweden is one example. The Swedish constitution, the Instrument of Government, says that the personal, economic and cultural welfare of the individual shall be fundamental aims of public activity. It also says that public institutions shall promote sustainable development leading to a good environment for present and future generations. It is the same picture as in Doughnut Economics: a social foundation that nobody should fall below and an ecological ceiling that no generation may break through.

That is why the vision can be shared by all parties. It says where society is going, but not how we get there. The path there is the task of politics, and it is discussed on the page [Elections and Parties](<../../Society Explorer/The Democratic System/Elections and Parties/Elections and Parties.md>) in the Democratic System. The Framework describes what sustainability requires and where the limits lie.

## From vision to understanding

The vision says where we are striving to go. To get there, we need to understand how society works and how its parts fit together. That picture is built up step by step on the page [Our Society](<../Our Society.md>), from the vision to the whole Society Map.

What the [Framework](<../../Society Explorer/Framework/Framework.md>) says about the limits needs to shape how we plan, prioritise and make decisions from the start. It is not just a way of checking afterwards whether development turned out to be sustainable.
