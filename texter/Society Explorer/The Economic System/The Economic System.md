The economic system is about how we organise work, production, exchange and the distribution of resources. It affects almost everything in everyday life: what is produced, how we work, what things cost and what opportunities different people get.

The economy is often presented as something technical, a system of figures, models and rules. But at its core the economy is a human system. It is shaped by decisions about what we produce, buy, own and invest in. It also depends on nature, on people's knowledge and trust and on democratic rules. That is why it cannot be understood in isolation from the rest of society.

But when you try to understand the economy, questions arise:

- Why does the economy have to grow?

- Where does money come from?

- Why do some people have so much more than others?

- Can the economy grow without harming the planet?

- And who actually decides where the economy is heading?

This is where TSS starts. Each group gathers questions that belong together and tries to give answers that make sense. The answers describe how today's system works, what consequences it has and what possibilities for change there are.
