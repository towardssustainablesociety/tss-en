## What does it mean for the economy to contribute to a sustainable society?

That the economy should help people live a good life within planetary boundaries. An economic system is a tool for organising work, resources, production, livelihoods and exchange. In a sustainable society, that tool needs to be used so that society's development fits within what nature can sustain.

Economic results can therefore not be judged only by how much is produced or how fast the economy grows. We also need to see what consequences the economy has for people, society and nature.

The economist Kate Raworth has described it as a doughnut, **Doughnut Economics**. The inner ring is the social foundation that every person needs to stand on. The outer ring is the ecological ceiling that we must not break through. The task of the economy is to keep us between the rings.

## Does the economy have to grow for society to develop?

No. Society can develop through better health, education, security, community, knowledge and well-functioning public services, without all development having to mean more production and consumption.

At the same time, economic growth is still an important part of how today's system works. A society that wants to reduce its dependence on growth therefore also needs to change the mechanisms that make work, livelihoods, investment and public finances dependent on continued economic expansion.

## Can the economy give people security without putting too much pressure on the planet?

Yes, in principle, but no country does so today. A study of over 150 countries, published in 2018, found that no country meets its people's basic needs with a level of resource use that would be sustainable if everyone lived that way. The study also showed that the physical basic needs, such as food, sanitation, electricity and a life free from extreme poverty, could probably be met for everyone within planetary boundaries.

That does not mean all economic activity must shrink. It means that the use of energy, materials and natural resources needs to fit within planetary boundaries, and that the economy needs to be directed at people's needs rather than at increased resource use in itself.

So security cannot be built in the long term on an economy that at the same time weakens society's ecological foundations.

## How is the economy connected with nature, people and democracy?

In every direction at once. The economy takes its resources from nature and returns waste and emissions. It is built on people's work, knowledge and trust, and it affects their living conditions in turn. And it is governed by laws and decisions made in democracy, while economic power influences which decisions are made.

This means a change in the economy always has consequences somewhere else. That is why the direction of the economy cannot be decided within the economy alone.

## What needs to change in the economy?

Several things at the same time, because the parts of the economy affect each other. There is no single change that solves every problem.

The changes may need to be about how resources are used, how work and livelihoods are organised, how investment is steered, how economic power is distributed and how the economy's consequences for people and nature are made visible.

An important starting point is to see the economy as part of society. Then it becomes possible to ask what the economy should contribute, instead of assuming that economic activity is the goal in itself.

## Who decides which direction the economy should take?

Nobody alone, but the direction is not given. Businesses, households, investors, banks and the public sector make decisions that together influence the direction of the economy. At the same time, political decisions set important frameworks for how the economy works.

Through democratic decisions, rules, investment, economic policy instruments and changed behaviour, society can influence which paths of development become possible.

## So what is the answer, really?

The economy does not have to be a goal in itself. It can be a tool for organising the resources, production and work needed for people to live a good life.

For a sustainable society, this means the development of the economy needs to be judged by what it contributes and what consequences it has. It is the whole that counts, not each individual activity: together, the economy's activities should improve people's lives and fit within planetary boundaries.

There is no ready-made model for what the economy of the future must look like. But the direction is becoming clearer: the economy needs to support society's development, not be a goal that society has to adapt to.

**So the question is not how we get society to adapt to the economy, but how we develop the economy so that it can serve a sustainable society.**
