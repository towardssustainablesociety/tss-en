## What does it mean for democracy to contribute to a sustainable society?

That it can lead society towards the vision, a good life for all within planetary boundaries, and remain a democracy at the same time. Decisions need to take account of nature, people's basic needs and future generations, and people need to remain able to influence and change those decisions.

Democracy has a special role among the four societal systems. It is where the direction of the others is set. The rules of the economy, the protection of nature and welfare are all the result of democratic decisions.

## How is democracy connected with nature, the economy and people?

It depends on all three. When nature is weakened there are more crises to handle, such as drought, floods and conflict over resources, and they put democracy under pressure. The economy provides the resources for what we share, but economic power also becomes political power, and a dependence on growth limits which decisions seem possible. And without trust, education and decent living conditions, people take part less and trust decisions less.

At the same time, democracy affects all three. It is democratic decisions that put a price on emissions, set taxes and build welfare. The dependence goes both ways, and that is why the health of democracy cannot be separated from that of the rest of society.

## Aren't authoritarian states better at the transition?

The answer is not simple. China is building solar and wind power faster than any other country and makes over 70 per cent of the world's electric cars. But China also accounts for over 30 per cent of the world's greenhouse gas emissions, and in 2024 almost 60 per cent of its electricity still came from coal.

Authoritarian states can make quick decisions, but also quick wrong decisions that nobody can stop. There is no free press to expose environmental scandals and no independent courts to protect those who are harmed. A transition that will take decades also needs people's support, not just their obedience.

## What needs to change in democracy?

Three things, which the other pages in the Democratic System describe. The direction needs to become shared and visible, with a vision that all parties share and parties that report their path towards it. The future needs a place in decisions, through measures, reports and representatives who keep the long-term consequences visible. And participation needs to become more equal and more real, so that decisions are supported by more people.

None of this requires a new democracy. It requires the one we have to be given what it lacks.

## Who decides which direction democracy should take?

The citizens, and nobody else. That is the core of democracy and what separates it from other forms of government. TSS can propose a vision and tools, but it is voters and their representatives who decide whether to use them.

That is why every change begins with people understanding how society fits together and wanting something from it.

## So what is the answer, really?

Democracy is the system where the direction of the other systems is set. It depends on nature, the economy and people's trust, and it affects them all. A sustainable society cannot be built without democracy, nor by a democracy that lacks direction.

Democracy is good at changing its leaders, but it lacks a way to set a direction and keep to it. The vision already has support, in the UN goals and, in Sweden, in the constitution. What is needed is to make it shared, to report the paths towards it, to give the future a voice and to let more people take part in deciding.

**The question is not whether democracy can manage the transition, but whether we give it the tools to lead it.**
