The democratic system is how society makes shared decisions. It is about who gets power and how that power is limited, how people can have a say, which rights protect them and how decisions become reality.

You can think of democracy as a factory for building society. Into it go the needs, experiences and will of citizens, together with knowledge about nature, the economy and people's living conditions. Out of it come laws, budgets, the work of public agencies and, over time, a different society. The citizens own the factory, and they appoint its management every four years. That does not mean citizens own everything in society, but through democracy they set the rules for all ownership.

The questions here follow a common thread: democracy is good at changing its leaders, but it lacks a way to set a direction and keep to it. That is not because politicians are worse than other people, but because of how the system is built. And that can be changed.

Some of the questions:

- What are we actually choosing when we vote?

- Who has power without being elected?

- Can the majority decide anything it likes?

- Why do falsehoods spread so easily?

- Who speaks for those not yet born?
