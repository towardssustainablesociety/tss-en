## What does culture have to do with sustainability?

More than you might think. Culture is not just art, music and literature. It is also the stories, habits and ways of seeing the world that we share. Culture influences what we see as normal, what we dream of and what we believe is possible.

That makes culture a force in the transition. Technology and laws are not enough if the story of a good life is still about having more. Culture can show other ways of living and make them understandable and attractive before they become common.

Cultural life is also an example of how basic needs can be met with a small footprint. Singing in a choir, acting in a play or reading a book gives community, creativity and meaning without using up many resources.

## What is the difference between education and learning?

Education is about learning to function within a system: a profession, a skill, a measurable result. **Learning** in the broader sense, what is called *bildning* in Swedish, is about understanding the system itself: connections, perspectives and judgement. One asks "what will you become?", the other "how do you understand the world?".

Both are needed. A society needs nurses, engineers and carpenters. But it also needs people who can see the connections between the economy, nature and democracy and who can test what they hear.

## Is school there for the labour market?

Partly, and that is reasonable. School should give young people the chance to make a living and contribute. The modern education system grew up when industrial society needed engineers, clerks and workers, and much of what is taught is still linked to the labour market.

The problem arises when what can be measured crowds out what cannot. Reflection, philosophy, history and an understanding of how society fits together easily get less room when schools are steered by grades, efficiency and competitiveness. That can produce people who are good at their jobs but find it hard to understand the big questions they must take a position on as citizens.

## Why is learning a question for democracy?

Because a democracy assumes that people can understand the questions they vote on. A sustainable society requires many decisions about complex matters: energy, the economy, climate and distribution. If only experts understand the connections, a dependence on authorities arises, and simplified stories easily take hold.

In Sweden, adult popular education (*folkbildning*), with study associations and folk high schools, has long had exactly that task: to give adults the chance to learn, discuss and take part, whatever their previous education. Public service broadcasting has had a similar educational mission.

More knowledge can sometimes create anxiety. Someone who begins to understand how interwoven the world is discovers uncertainties and conflicts that were previously hidden. But the alternative to naive trust is not distrust of everything. It is the ability to test, compare and change one's mind.

## What happens to learning when information becomes free?

It changes character. Information is everywhere today, and AI can explain almost anything, tailored to each person. That is a great opportunity for lifelong learning.

But information is not the same as understanding. Social media and news feeds reward speed, emotion and conflict rather than context and reflection. Then judgement becomes more important than facts: the ability to tell the important from the unimportant and the credible from the doubtful. The teacher's role may therefore become less about passing on information and more about guiding, questioning and creating context.

How information and public debate affect democracy is covered in the Democratic System.

## So what is the answer, really?

Culture and learning shape how we understand the world and what we believe is possible. Culture can make new ways of living understandable and attractive, and cultural life is in itself a way of living richly with a small footprint.

Learning gives people the chance to understand connections, test claims and take part in decisions about society's future. It becomes more important, not less, when information becomes free and the questions become more complex.

**The question is not only what we need to know to get a job, but what we need to understand to shape society together.**
