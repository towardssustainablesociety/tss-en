The social and cultural system is about how we live together. It includes our relationships and communities, our norms and values, our culture and learning and the living conditions that decide what opportunities people get.

Much of what holds a society together does not show up in any statistics. Trust, the sense of belonging and the will to contribute are rarely measured in money, but without them neither the economy nor democracy works. This is also where several of the hardest questions of the transition lie: what we need to live well, what we owe each other and how we handle wanting different things.

But when you try to understand how we live together, questions arise:

- What does a person need to live a good life?

- Do we become happier by getting richer?

- Is someone a bad citizen if they are content with little?

- Why do we trust each other, and what happens when trust disappears?

- And how can we live together when we are so different?

This is where TSS starts. Each group gathers questions that belong together and tries to give answers that make sense. The answers describe how things are today, why they are that way and what could be different.

Choose a question that interests you from the list at the end of the page, and start there.
