## What does it mean for the social and cultural system to contribute to a sustainable society?

That people can live good lives together, in a way that lasts. It means that nobody falls below the social foundation, that people can trust each other and feel that they belong, and that society can handle differences and disagreements without anyone being shut out.

The social system is also what makes the transition possible. Technical solutions and political decisions are not enough if people do not trust each other, do not see the change as fair or cannot imagine another way of living.

## Aren't technology and the economy enough?

No. Technology can make most things more efficient, and economic policy instruments can change prices. But a reform that is seen as unfair will not get support, however good it looks on paper. And a technology that makes something cheaper can lead to us using more of it.

What decides the matter is often what we value, whether we trust each other and whether we see the change as our own. Those are questions for the social and cultural system.

## How is the social connected with nature, the economy and democracy?

In every direction at once. Nature sets the limits for how we can meet our needs. The economy organises work and distributes resources, and with them many of people's living conditions. Democracy decides the shared rules and is society's way of handling tensions.

At the same time, the social affects all the others. Our norms steer what we consume and therefore how hard nature is pressed. Trust makes the economy cheaper and democracy stronger. And unequal living conditions can become political tensions that make every other decision harder.

## What needs to change?

First, the view of a good life. Basic needs need to be at the centre, and a good life needs to be measurable in something other than consumption. That means making sure nobody falls below the foundation, and that the room above it is shared out in a way the planet can bear.

Second, the view of work and contribution. Reciprocity concerns the contribution to the foundation, and unpaid work needs to be counted. The responsibility for there being sustainable jobs lies with society, not with the individual.

And third, the fairness of the transition. A change that increases injustice or destroys trust will not last. Education, openness and real opportunities to have influence are preconditions for it to win support.

## How do we know whether we are going in the right direction?

By measuring what matters to people, not just what shows up in GDP. Do people have their basic needs met? Can children get different chances from their parents? Do people trust each other and the institutions? Are they doing well?

Such measures need to be followed over time and set against the ecological limits. When things go in the wrong direction, society needs to be able to correct its course. How the economy's success can be measured in ways other than GDP is covered in Growth and Development in the Economic System.

## Who decides which direction the social system should take?

Everyone and no one. Norms and culture are shaped in millions of everyday encounters, and nobody can order trust or community into being. But some have more influence than others: politics through laws and welfare, schools and the media through what they teach and highlight, and businesses through advertising and what they offer.

In a democracy, it is in the end the citizens who decide. That is why the direction of the social system is so closely linked with how well people understand society and can influence it.

## So what is the answer, really?

A sustainable social and cultural system makes it possible for people to live secure and meaningful lives, contribute in different ways and handle differences without anyone being left out. The foundation is that everyone has their basic needs met, and that this is exactly what we owe each other: to contribute to the foundation if we can.

The social system is not a side issue in the transition. It is where the transition becomes possible or impossible. Trust, fairness and a shared picture of a good life decide whether the technical and economic changes win support.

**The question is not how we get people to adapt to the transition, but how we build a society where it is easy to live well together within planetary boundaries.**
