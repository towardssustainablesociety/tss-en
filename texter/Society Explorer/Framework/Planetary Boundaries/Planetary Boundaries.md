Planetary Boundaries is a scientific framework that describes limits for the Earth's stability and resilience. It was developed in 2009 by a group of researchers led by Johan Rockström at the Stockholm Resilience Centre. The framework identifies nine processes in the Earth system that together keep the Earth stable and that are affected by human activity.

The idea is that there is a **safe operating space** for humanity within these boundaries. If human pressure crosses a boundary, the risk increases of changes that could weaken the Earth's capacity to support human life and the development of society. The boundaries are also connected, so that a change in one area can affect other parts of the Earth system.

The nine planetary boundaries concern:

- climate change

- biodiversity loss (biosphere integrity)

- land-system change

- freshwater

- nitrogen and phosphorus flows (biogeochemical flows)

- ocean acidification

- atmospheric aerosols

- the ozone layer

- novel entities: new chemical substances and other substances created by humans

All nine boundaries were assessed for the first time in a scientific review in 2023. At that point six of the nine had been crossed. According to the Planetary Health Check 2025, seven of the nine have now been crossed, since ocean acidification has also passed its boundary. The state of the planet is described in more detail on the page [State of the Planet](<../../The Ecological System/State of the Planet/State of the Planet.md>) in the Ecological System.

## Planetary Boundaries in TSS

For TSS, Planetary Boundaries describes a basic condition for the vision of a sustainable society: society's development needs to fit within the planet's ecological limits.

Planetary Boundaries complements the UN SDGs and Doughnut Economics in the Framework. The SDGs help describe what sustainable development needs to cover. Planetary Boundaries describes the ecological conditions that development needs to stay within. Doughnut Economics ties the ecological dimension together with people's needs and uses the planetary boundaries as its ecological ceiling.

Planetary Boundaries also has a special role in TSS. It is part of the Framework, but at the same time concrete knowledge within the Ecological System. There it comes back when we explore how society's activities affect nature's processes and what dependencies there are.

Planetary Boundaries is therefore not a limit on human development, but a way of understanding the ecological conditions that all human development depends on.
