A sustainable society needs a shared basis for understanding what sustainable development means and within what limits it can take place.

TSS starts from the vision of a sustainable society and uses the global sustainability areas of the UN SDGs as an important basis for describing what such development needs to cover.

The Framework is complemented by Doughnut Economics, which shows how people's needs can be reconciled with the planet's ecological limits, and Planetary Boundaries, which describes the ecological conditions that all human development depends on.

Together, the three parts give a shared basis for understanding and assessing the development of society in the direction of the vision of a sustainable society.

The Framework describes what sustainability requires and where the limits lie. That the vision also has a democratic mandate, through the UN and through national constitutions such as Sweden's, is described on the page [The Vision](<../../Our Society/The Vision/The Vision.md>).

The Framework is not a fifth societal system. It sits above the four societal systems and can be used to understand how they together contribute to the development of society.

## The three parts of the Framework

- [The UN SDGs](<The UN SDGs/The UN SDGs.md>) – what sustainable development of society needs to cover.

- [Doughnut Economics](<Doughnut Economics/Doughnut Economics.md>) – how people's needs and planetary boundaries can be understood together.

- [Planetary Boundaries](<Planetary Boundaries/Planetary Boundaries.md>) – which ecological conditions need to be respected.

The three parts complement each other and are linked to People and the four societal systems.
