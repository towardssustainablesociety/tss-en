Doughnut Economics is a way of describing the conditions for development in which people can meet their needs without overshooting the planet's ecological limits. The model was developed by the British economist Kate Raworth.

The model consists of two limits, usually drawn as a doughnut. The inner edge, the **social foundation**, describes the basic needs that every person needs to have met in order to live a dignified life, such as food, water, health, education, housing, energy and the chance to have a say. The foundation builds on the social minimum standards that the world's governments agreed on in the UN SDGs. Below the social foundation lies social shortfall.

The outer edge of the doughnut, the **ecological ceiling**, describes the limits that humanity needs to stay within so as not to seriously damage the Earth's life-support systems. The ceiling is made up of the nine planetary boundaries. Above the ceiling lies ecological overshoot.

Between the social foundation and the ecological ceiling lies what Doughnut Economics calls a **safe and just space** for humanity. It is within this space that people can meet their needs while the planet's life-support systems are preserved.

## Doughnut Economics in TSS

In TSS, Doughnut Economics complements the UN SDGs and Planetary Boundaries in the Framework. The SDGs help describe which areas sustainable development of society needs to cover. Planetary Boundaries describes the ecological conditions that need to be respected. Doughnut Economics ties the social and ecological dimensions together and shows the space between them.

In this way the model gives a perspective on the basic challenge: creating good living conditions for all within the planet's ecological limits. It is the same picture as in the vision of a sustainable society: a good life for all, within planetary boundaries.

When TSS talks about basic needs, it is the social foundation that is meant. Sustainability is not decided by which needs people have, but by how those needs are met.

Doughnut Economics is not a parallel model of society in TSS. It is part of the Framework and can be used to understand and assess how different parts of society relate to both people's needs and planetary boundaries. In the Society Map, the model can be related to People and to the four societal systems.
